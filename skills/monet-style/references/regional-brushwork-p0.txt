Organize strokes by material and depth instead of covering the whole canvas with a uniform texture. SKY AND MIST: broad, interconnected low-contrast marks with quiet transitions, not an even scatter of short dabs. WATER: directional horizontal broken strokes following ripples and reflections; concentrate small contrasting accents only where optical light demands them. GRASS AND FOLIAGE: clusters of varied length and density following growth direction, with breathing space between clusters. FABRIC AND FIGURES: longer gestures that follow folds, wind and motion; simplify minor edges without erasing required subjects. BUILDINGS AND GRAINSTACKS: connected light-and-shadow masses and material-aligned marks instead of countless decorative chips. Across foreground, middle ground and distance, vary mark scale and density deliberately; avoid repeated fish-scale tessellations and false fine oil-paint texture. Preserve the existing color, lighting, atmosphere, scene, counts and framing; do not solve artifacts by blurring the whole image.
